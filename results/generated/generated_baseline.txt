Topic: a story about an unusual holiday trip

Text: 
Last summer, Mark planned an unusual holiday trip to a small island. He was nervous at first—no one had visited the island before, and the locals were very polite but intelligent in a strange way. "Why are you here?" they asked, smiling. Mark explained he wanted adventure, but they just laughed. "You’ll see," they said.

On the first day, Mark saw a creative market with colorful clothes and attractive jewelry. He bought a hat, surprised by how happy it made him. Later, he tried local food and felt scared when he saw a huge, strange fruit. "Don’t eat it!" a boy warned. Mark was worried, but he didn’t. The fruit was sweet, and he realized the island was magical.

By the end, Mark wasn’t lazy anymore—he had fallen in love with the place. "Next year," he promised, "I’ll bring friends!"

Questions: 
Q1: Why was Mark nervous at first about the trip?
Correct: He had never heard of the island before.
Distractors: He was afraid of the locals / He didn’t like strange food / He was too lazy to travel.
Explanation:
- Correct: The text states Mark was nervous because "no one had visited the island before" meaning it was unknown and possibly risky.
- Distractors:
  - "Afraid of the locals" → The locals were polite and intelligent, not threatening.
  - "Didn’t like strange food" → Mark’s fear of food came later (the strange fruit), not his initial nerves.
  - "Too lazy to travel" → The text says he "wasn’t lazy anymore" after the trip, so laziness wasn’t his issue at the start.
---
Q2: What did Mark buy at the market that made him happy?
Correct: A colorful hat.
Distractors: An intelligent book / Strange fruit / Polite souvenirs.
Explanation:
- Correct: The text says he bought a hat at the "creative" market, and it made him happy.
- Distractors:
  - "Intelligent book" → The word "intelligent" describes the locals, not an item.
  - "Strange fruit" → He saw the fruit but didn’t buy it (and was scared of it).
  - "Polite souvenirs" → The text doesn’t mention souvenirs; "polite" refers to the locals’ behavior.
---
Q3: Why did the boy warn Mark about the strange fruit?
Correct: He knew it was magical and dangerous to eat.
Distractors: He was scared of Mark / The fruit was rotten / The boy was lazy.
Explanation:
- Correct: The island was magical, and the fruit’s description ("huge, strange") implies it had unknown effects. The boy’s warning suggests it was special (not just rotten).
- Distractors:
  - "Scared of Mark" → The boy was helpful, not afraid.
  - "The fruit was rotten" → The text doesn’t say it was bad; Mark later found it sweet.  
  - "The boy was lazy" → The word "lazy" is used to describe Mark’s change in attitude, not the boy.
