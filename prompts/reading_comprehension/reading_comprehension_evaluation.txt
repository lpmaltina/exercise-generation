Evaluate the reading comprehension exercise using the following criteria.

Text: {text}
Generated exercise: {exercise}

Part 1: For each question, assign a score of 1 to 3 for each criterion below and explain your reasoning.

Clarity & Grammar: Is the question clear and grammatically correct?
1: The question is confusing or contains grammatical errors that hinder understanding.
2: The question is mostly clear, but has minor issues in wording or grammar that do not significantly affect comprehension.
3: The question is clear, well-worded, and grammatically flawless.

Text-Based Answerability: Can the question be answered using only the information provided in the text?
1: The answer is not found in the text at all.
2: The answer is partially supported by the text.
3: The answer is fully present in the text, or directly inferred from it.

Reading Dependency. How difficult would it be to guess the correct answer without reading the text?
1: The correct answer is easily guessable using only general knowledge.
2: The correct answer is possibly guessable using general knowledge.
3: The correct answer cannot be guessed without reading the text.

Answer Unambiguity. Is exactly one answer unambiguously correct based on the text?
1: Multiple answers are correct, or the intended "correct" answer is contradicted by the text.
2: One answer is clearly intended as correct, but another option can be considered partially correct.
3: Exactly one answer is unambiguously correct.

Distractor Plausibility. Are all incorrect options believable to someone who has read the text but not fully understood it?
1: All incorrect options are obviously wrong.
2: Some incorrect options are obviously wrong.
3: All incorrect options are plausible for someone who read but did not fully understand the text.

Part 2: Evaluate the overall exercise.

Text coverage. Do the questions cover different parts of the text and are not concentrated in one part?
1: All or most questions target the same part of the text.
2: Questions cover different parts of the text, but one part is overrepresented or neglected.
3: Questions are evenly distributed across the text, covering different ideas.

Exercise usability. How ready the exercise is for classroom or self-study use:
1: Cannot be used.
2: Can be used, but requires some minor alterations.
3: Can be used as is.
